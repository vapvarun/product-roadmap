## THREAD 3: Guest Voting Deep Dive

**Hook Tweet:**
Want 10x more feedback on your product roadmap?

Enable guest voting.

Here's why it's a game-changer 🧵

---

**Tweet 2:**
The friction problem:

Most voting systems require login.
Login requires account creation.
Account creation requires email, password, verification.

Each step loses 50%+ of potential voters.

---

**Tweet 3:**
The math:

100 visitors see your roadmap
50 would vote if it was easy
5 actually create an account

You're getting 5% of possible votes.

---

**Tweet 4:**
Guest voting changes this:

100 visitors see your roadmap
50 would vote if it was easy
45 actually vote

You're getting 90% of possible votes.

---

**Tweet 5:**
"But what about spam and gaming?"

Valid concern. Here's how to handle it:

- Track by IP address (one vote per person)
- Rate limiting prevents abuse
- Patterns flag suspicious activity

It works.

---

**Tweet 6:**
"But I want to know who's voting"

Counterpoint: Do you really?

Anonymous voting = honest voting.

People vote for what they want, not what they think you want to hear.

---

**Tweet 7:**
Real talk:

The goal isn't to know who voted.
The goal is to know WHAT to build.

Guest voting gives you 10x more signal.

More signal = better decisions.

## THREAD 4: Suggestion Workflow

**Hook Tweet:**
Your customers have feature ideas you'd never think of.

But you'll never hear them unless you make it easy.

Here's the system I recommend 🧵

---

**Tweet 2:**
Most feature requests come through:

- Support tickets (mixed with bugs)
- Email (scattered)
- Social media (lost in noise)
- Sales calls (remembered inconsistently)

None of these are designed for feature collection.

---

**Tweet 3:**
What you need: A dedicated suggestion system.

Requirements:
✅ Easy to submit (low friction)
✅ Easy to moderate (you stay in control)
✅ Easy to discover (others can vote)

---

**Tweet 4:**
The workflow:

1. Customer clicks "Suggest Feature"
2. Fills out simple form (title + description)
3. Suggestion goes to pending queue
4. You review and decide: approve, edit, or decline
5. Approved suggestions become voteable items

---

**Tweet 5:**
Key insight: The review step is crucial.

Not every suggestion belongs on your public roadmap.

- Some are bugs (redirect to support)
- Some are duplicates (merge them)
- Some don't fit your vision (decline gracefully)

You stay in control.

---

**Tweet 6:**
What happens when you approve a suggestion:

- It appears on your public roadmap
- Other customers can vote on it
- Discussion can happen in comments
- You might discover it's your most-requested feature

---

**Tweet 7:**
Real example:

A customer suggested an integration I'd never heard of.

After research, I realized it was popular in their industry.

We built it. Became a top acquisition driver.

I would have never known without the suggestion system.

---

**Tweet 8:**
Tools for this:

- Product Roadmap (WordPress, free)
- Canny (SaaS)
- Productboard (Enterprise)
- Linear (has public roadmap features)

Pick what fits your stack. The approach matters more than the tool.
