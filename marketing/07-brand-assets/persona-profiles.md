# Product Roadmap - Target Persona Profiles

## Overview

This document defines the primary target personas for Product Roadmap marketing. Use these profiles to guide content creation, feature prioritization, and marketing messaging.

---

## Persona 1: Product Manager Paula

### Demographics
- **Role:** Product Manager at a SaaS company
- **Age:** 28-40
- **Team Size:** Works with 5-15 person development team
- **Industry:** B2B or B2C software, typically startup or mid-size
- **Technical Level:** Medium - comfortable with tools but not a developer

### Goals
- Prioritize feature development based on customer demand
- Reduce time spent collecting and organizing feedback
- Communicate roadmap to stakeholders clearly
- Make data-driven product decisions
- Keep customers engaged and informed

### Pain Points
- Feature requests scattered across email, Slack, support tickets
- Hard to know which features customers actually want most
- Stakeholders asking "what are we building and why?"
- Customers feeling unheard when features aren't shipped
- Difficulty showing ROI of product decisions

### Current Solutions
- Spreadsheets for tracking requests
- Trello or Jira for internal planning
- Occasional customer surveys
- Manual vote counting in forums

### Why Product Roadmap Appeals
- **Voting system** gives real data on customer priorities
- **Public roadmap** reduces stakeholder questions
- **Analytics** (Pro) provides presentation-ready data
- **Suggestion workflow** organizes incoming feedback

### Objections to Address
- "We already use [tool] for product planning"
- "Our roadmap is confidential"
- "Will customers expect everything they vote for?"
- "How do I migrate existing feature requests?"

### Key Messages for Paula
> "Stop guessing which features to build. Product Roadmap lets customers vote on what matters most, giving you the data to make confident prioritization decisions."

### Content She Responds To
- Case studies showing prioritization improvements
- ROI calculations and time savings
- Integration guides with existing tools
- Best practices for public roadmap management

## Persona 2: Developer Dave

### Demographics
- **Role:** WordPress Plugin/Theme Developer or Freelancer
- **Age:** 25-45
- **Business:** Solo or small team (1-5 people)
- **Industry:** WordPress ecosystem
- **Technical Level:** High - writes code daily

### Goals
- Share development plans with plugin/theme users
- Collect feature requests in an organized way
- Reduce repetitive support questions about "when will X be added?"
- Build community around products
- Look professional and established

### Pain Points
- Users submit duplicate feature requests constantly
- No visibility into what users actually want most
- Support forums cluttered with feature requests
- Feeling disconnected from user needs
- Limited time for community management

### Current Solutions
- GitHub Issues (for technical users only)
- Support plugin forums
- Trello board (often not public)
- Email inbox
- No system at all

### Why Product Roadmap Appeals
- **Native WordPress** integration (shortcode, block)
- **Free version** covers basic needs
- **Guest voting** works for non-technical users
- **REST API** for custom integration possibilities
- **Developer friendly** with hooks and filters

### Objections to Address
- "I can build this myself"
- "GitHub Issues is free"
- "My users are developers, they can use GitHub"
- "Is it lightweight? I care about performance"

### Key Messages for Dave
> "Give your users a voice with a native WordPress roadmap. Easy shortcode integration, guest voting for non-technical users, and full REST API for when you want to customize."

### Content He Responds To
- Technical documentation and API reference
- Performance benchmarks
- Code examples and hook/filter lists
- Comparison with GitHub Issues/DIY approaches

## Persona 3: Founder Fiona

### Demographics
- **Role:** Founder/CEO of early-stage startup
- **Age:** 30-50
- **Company Stage:** Seed to Series A, 10-50 employees
- **Industry:** SaaS, typically WordPress-based or WP as marketing site
- **Technical Level:** Low to medium - focuses on business, not tech

### Goals
- Build products customers love (and will pay for)
- Create transparency with customers
- Reduce churn by showing responsiveness
- Build community and engagement
- Look professional despite small team

### Pain Points
- Customers churning because "features I need aren't coming"
- Hard to know what to build with limited resources
- Competitors seem more responsive to customers
- Engineering team working on wrong priorities
- No time to manually track and respond to feedback

### Current Solutions
- Customer success team collecting feedback manually
- Intercom or Zendesk for support (but not roadmap)
- Internal notion/wiki for planning
- Occasional NPS surveys

### Why Product Roadmap Appeals
- **Transparency** builds trust and reduces churn
- **Voting** surfaces true priorities
- **Professional appearance** without big investment
- **Analytics** (Pro) for board/investor presentations
- **Quick setup** respects limited time

### Objections to Address
- "We need enterprise features"
- "Is this secure enough for our customers?"
- "Can we white-label it?"
- "What about our existing support tool integration?"

### Key Messages for Fiona
> "Turn customer feedback into your competitive advantage. Product Roadmap creates transparency that builds trust, while voting data ensures you're building what customers actually want."

### Content She Responds To
- Business outcomes and case studies
- Churn reduction statistics
- Quick-start guides
- Integration possibilities
- Enterprise/security considerations

## Persona 4: Agency Owner Alex

### Demographics
- **Role:** Owner/Director at WordPress agency
- **Age:** 35-55
- **Team Size:** 5-30 person agency
- **Industry:** WordPress development, web agencies
- **Technical Level:** Medium - manages technical people

### Goals
- Keep clients informed on project progress
- Reduce "what's the status?" meetings and calls
- Manage multiple client projects efficiently
- Look professional and organized
- Document project decisions and changes

### Pain Points
- Clients constantly asking for status updates
- Multiple clients, multiple projects, hard to track
- No good way to show visual project timelines
- Feature scope creep without documentation
- Time wasted in status meetings

### Current Solutions
- Basecamp or Asana for project management
- Weekly status email updates
- Client calls and meetings
- Custom client portals (expensive to build)

### Why Product Roadmap Appeals
- **Multi-product** (Pro) for different clients
- **Timeline view** (Pro) for visual project planning
- **Client-facing** professional interface
- **Reduces meetings** with self-service status checks
- **Comments** for documented discussions

### Objections to Address
- "We already have project management tools"
- "Can clients have their own login?"
- "Is this per-site or multisite?"
- "Can we brand it for our agency?"

### Key Messages for Alex
> "Give every client their own roadmap with Pro's multi-product support. Timeline views show progress visually, while voting helps prioritize scope discussions."

### Content He Responds To
- Multi-project management guides
- Agency workflow case studies
- Client communication templates
- White-labeling/branding possibilities
- Multisite compatibility info

## Interview Questions for Each Persona

Use these questions when gathering testimonials or conducting customer research:

### For Product Managers
1. How were you collecting feature feedback before Product Roadmap?
2. Has the voting data changed how you prioritize features?
3. What was stakeholder reaction to the public roadmap?
4. How much time has it saved in feedback organization?

### For Developers
1. How does it compare to using GitHub Issues?
2. What customizations have you made with hooks/API?
3. Has it changed how you communicate with users?
4. What would you tell other developers considering it?

### For Founders
1. How has transparency affected customer trust?
2. What feedback have customers given about the roadmap?
3. Has it influenced retention or churn metrics?
4. How do you use voting data in planning meetings?

### For Agencies
1. How do you organize multiple client roadmaps?
2. Has it reduced client status meetings?
3. What was client reaction to seeing their roadmap?
4. How does it fit into your existing workflow?

### For eCommerce
1. How engaged are customers with voting?
2. What product decisions has it influenced?
3. How does it fit with your WooCommerce setup?
4. What would you tell other store owners about it?
